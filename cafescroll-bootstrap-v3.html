A scroll feed for independent cafes

cafescroll.com

The domain inspired the idea. Here's the starting point.

Browse independent cafes visually — photos, vibes, the feeling of a space. Local spots surface first, the rest of the world behind them. Over time, the feed learns what draws your attention using the same approach Spotify uses for music: behavioral signals across users, not ratings or surveys. The overlap between what you pay attention to and what similar users pay attention to becomes the recommendation.

The model

Spotify, but for places

Spotify
Figures out what music you'd like
Watches what you play, skip, save, and replay. Finds users with overlapping taste. The gap between your listening history and theirs becomes your Discover Weekly. No surveys — the algorithm learns from attention.

Same mechanism, different domain

CafeScroll
Figures out what cafes you'd love
Tracks what catches your attention — what you pause on, tap into, save. Users with similar taste patterns reveal cafes to each other. The aesthetic and vibe of the space is the signal, not star ratings or review text.

The focus is independent spots with actual character. Not chains. The individually unique places — where the light hits a specific way, where the counter wear tells you people keep coming back. The places worth discovering that you'd walk past without knowing.

The vibe is anti-doomscroll. Browsing at a pace where the browsing itself is the point — closer to window-shopping a street you've never been down than refreshing a feed.

Where things stand

Honest starting point

Domain cafescroll.com — owned, no prior history Content Google Maps has cafe photos. Cafe websites, Instagram, Yelp have more. The visual content exists scattered across the internet — it needs to be surfaced in one place. Design Warm, calm, not sterile. Should feel like being in a good cafe, not like using an app. Idea A good first map. It'll get redrawn once it hits reality — but the starting point is clear and the first steps are small.
Why this has legs

The starting point is unusually easy. A beautiful scroll feed of real cafes in one city can exist with minimal infrastructure — Google Maps API, a React frontend, and some care with the visual presentation. Each step after that has a natural incentive for someone to participate (cafe owners get free exposure, users get discovery, the platform gets content). And the recommendation model improves automatically as more people use it.

A first roadmap

Where it goes from there

Start Visual Discovery
The scroll feed. One city, curated photos. Proves the concept works and the curation process is repeatable for other cities.
Growth More content, more cities
Cafe owners submit their own photos — free exposure is an obvious incentive. Users who are already posting cafe content to social media have a place to put it that's actually built for it. The content pipeline starts solving itself.
Platform Cafe Profiles
Owner-managed profiles with menus, hours, their own photos. Loyalty programs. Tools for the cafe-customer relationship.

This is where it stops being an aggregator and becomes a two-sided platform. The cafe has a reason to maintain their presence because it's directly useful to them. Not a listing on someone else's site — a tool they actually want to use.

Network Sister Cafes
Users with similar taste reveal cafe-to-cafe affinities. A shop in Melbourne discovers it shares a vibe-twin in Lisbon.

This falls out of the preference data naturally — the clusters self-organize. Cafe-to-cafe connections between owners who'll never compete because they're on different continents. Shared advice, tips, friendships. Travelers and digital nomads planning routes between vibe-matched spots. Content creators cafe-hopping between them — which feeds right back into growth.

The feature that makes this more than a nicer Yelp. A network effect between businesses — most platforms only get that between users.

Revenue Monetization
Premium cafe tools — analytics, enhanced profiles, mobile ordering. Direct-from-cafe purchasing: see a cafe on the other side of the world, order their exact coffee to get a taste of the vibe from home. Subscriptions, merch.

Multiple revenue paths, each one useful to someone. The coffee subscription model that works for YouTubers, applied to individual cafes with actual character. Not one monetization bet — several natural ones that emerge from the platform being useful.

Getting started

What this needs

The idea, the domain, the product direction, the willingness to do the non-code work — curation, outreach to cafes, content, relationship-building — that's here. What's needed is someone comfortable building a React + TypeScript frontend who cares about how things look and feel.

This is a visual platform. It has to look right from day one. Mobile-first, warm aesthetic, photos as the primary content. The person building it needs to care about that part, not just make it functional.

One person visits a cafe they wouldn't have found otherwise.

That's the win condition. Everything after that is iteration.
